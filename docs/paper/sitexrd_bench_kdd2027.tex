\begin{abstract}
\begin{revision}
Accurate reconstruction of a powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern does not
by itself establish correct recovery of its latent structure. We present
\dataset{}, a first-stage benchmark toward alloy powder-XRD refinement.
Its two complementary subproblems are HEA-SRO for short-range chemical-order
regression in high-entropy alloys, and
Gamma-Decomp (gamma/gamma-prime decomposition) for recovering unordered,
phase- and reflection-specific components from overlapping peaks. The release
contains 84,019 structure--XRD pairs, 10,000 mixtures, and 21,116 overlap
windows, with traceable structure--reflection--component--observation
provenance. Same-composition controls and grouped in-distribution (ID) and
out-of-distribution (OOD) splits test shortcut use and generalization.
Task-specific metrics evaluate parent-macro SRO error and matched component
recovery separately from whole-pattern reconstruction; no cross-track score
is used. Composition-only, peak-feature, and neural baselines provide
complementary reference points rather than assuming that a more complex
model must perform better. After correcting zero-count SRO targets and
retraining, the Spectrum Transformer achieves an in-distribution
parent-macro mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.4156 versus 0.5310 for Composition
Ridge. A same-learner feature ablation reduces error by 19.90--22.56\%
when adding peak descriptors to composition across four frozen splits.
These clean-simulation gains do not establish direct experimental SRO
observability; fixed-cell and perturbation diagnostics expose important
limitations. The revised Gamma evaluation contract is documented, but its
complete corrected result matrix remains pending.
The current release provides computational latent labels; paired experimental
SRO and independently annotated component targets are not included.
Its experimental patterns support phase-level and whole-pattern checks.
These resources prepare data and evaluation protocols for future refinement
models, rather than demonstrating a complete refinement pipeline.
\ifdefined\anonymouscopy
Code and benchmark artifacts are described in the availability statement.
\else
Code and benchmark protocols are available at
\url{https://github.com/guohajining-cmd/alloy-xrd-bench}.
\fi
\end{revision}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Powder XRD is non-destructive, scalable, and sensitive to long-range crystal
structure, but its inverse problem is underdetermined: a measured
one-dimensional pattern aggregates many reflections and can be compatible with
multiple latent structures. This matters when the target is local chemical
order or phase-resolved components rather than a coarse phase label.

\begin{revision}
The motivating failure case is concrete. In the public high-entropy-alloy
(HEA) training split, two Co$_{0.25}$Cr$_{0.25}$Fe$_{0.25}$Ni$_{0.25}$
face-centered cubic (FCC) structures have XRD cosine similarity 0.9967 but
corrected first/second-shell short-range-order (SRO) $L_1$ distance 26.67
(Figure~\ref{fig:hea_sro_example}). We retain the original released pair;
we do not reselect it after correcting the labels. A model can fit the
dominant Bragg pattern and still miss the structure-derived local-order
target. Similarly, a small residual for a \gphase{}/\gp{} mixture does not
establish correct assignment to phase and Miller-index (HKL) components.
\dataset{} distinguishes observation matching from recovery of generated
latent targets, not simulated consistency from experimentally proven truth.
\end{revision}

Two alloy settings motivate complementary tests. High-entropy alloys (HEAs)
extend alloy design to multiple principal elements
~\cite{yeh2004hea,cantor2004multicomponent}. Their structure, phase behavior,
and modelling are reviewed in
~\cite{pickering2016hea,gorsse2018hea,widom2014hea,miracle2017hea,george2019hea}.
\begin{revision}
Experimental SRO studies include microscopy observations in FCC
CrCoNi~\cite{zhang2020sro} and atom-probe-based
quantification~\cite{he2024apt}. These studies establish the existence of
experimental SRO research, not unique SRO recovery from conventional powder XRD.
\end{revision}
Our same-composition controls test that distinction under a declared forward
model. Ni-based superalloy metallurgy provides the second setting
~\cite{reed2006superalloys,pollock2006superalloys,sims1987superalloys,durand1997superalloys}:
the released mixtures test separation of related \gphase{}/\gp{} reflection
profiles under simulated broadening and acquisition perturbations.

\begin{revision}
The shared long-term objective is alloy powder-XRD refinement. This benchmark
is a first step toward that objective, providing data, targets, and evaluation
protocols for future refinement-oriented models. HEA-SRO and Gamma-Decomp
address complementary subproblems, local chemical-order information and
reflection-resolved components. They are scored separately at this stage;
we do not present a completed joint refinement algorithm.
\dataset{} separates observational fit from latent correctness and contributes
(1) a shared structure--reflection--component--observation hierarchy, (2)
same-composition HEA controls and traceable \gphase{}/\gp{} mixtures, (3)
task-specific permutation-invariant metrics, and (4) grouped ID/OOD protocols,
identifiability diagnostics, and parent-aware uncertainty estimates. Strength
and creep-life prediction are outside the benchmark contract.
\end{revision}

\section{Related Work and Positioning}

Classical powder-diffraction procedures~\cite{klug1974xray} and Rietveld
refinement~\cite{rietveld1969,young1993rietveld} provide the measurement and
whole-pattern modelling background. Pseudo-Voigt line shapes model diffraction
broadening~\cite{thompson1987tch}, while crystallography packages support
standardized profile refinement~\cite{toby2013gsasii,lutterotti2010maud}.
Small profile residuals alone do not establish a correct structural
model~\cite{toby2006rvalues}. Warren--Cowley coefficients
summarize conditional neighbor probabilities without assigning arbitrary atom
indices~\cite{cowley1950}.

XRD learning has addressed symmetry classification, augmentation, and
multiphase identification or quantification
~\cite{vecsei2019symmetry,oviedo2019xrd,lee2020multiphase,greasley2023multiphase,szymanski2021crabnet},
while set prediction supports unordered component outputs
~\cite{lee2019settransformer,carion2020detr}. Confidence calibration
~\cite{guo2017calibration} and proper probabilistic scoring
~\cite{gneiting2007proper} concern the quality of uncertainty estimates.
Domain-generalization benchmarks and uncertainty-under-shift evaluations
motivate assessing controlled shifts beyond random-split accuracy
~\cite{gulrajani2021domainbed,koh2021wilds,ovadia2019uncertainty}.

\begin{revision}
Among the resources considered in this study, benchmark-ready experimental
data pairing powder-XRD observations with independently quantified,
sample-matched SRO targets are rare. This is a scoped assessment, not an
exhaustive dataset census. The current release does not include these
experimental SRO labels or independently annotated reflection-level components.
Its computational targets provide a controlled starting point for future
refinement-model development, complementing the experimental studies above.
\end{revision}

\section{Dataset Overview}

Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_overview} summarizes the two data-generation chains.

For latent material state $z$ and acquisition condition $n$, the release stores
\begin{equation}
z\longrightarrow\mathcal{R}(z)\longrightarrow
\{c_k(\theta;z,n)\}_{k=1}^{K}\longrightarrow y(\theta),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}$ is the indexed reflection set and $c_k$ is a broadened
phase/HKL component. The observation is
\begin{equation}
y(\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_k(\theta;z,n)+b(\theta;n)+\epsilon(\theta;n).
\end{equation}
Stable sample, structure-state, parent-group, reflection, and component IDs link
every level to a frozen manifest, configuration, checksum, and provenance row.

\textbf{Data significance.}
The release is useful not merely because it contains additional XRD curves, but
because it preserves the hidden causes of those curves. HEA-SRO links spectra to
site-resolved chemical order, so users can test whether a model recovers local
arrangement rather than composition shortcuts. Gamma-Decomp stores phase/HKL
components before they are summed into an observed pattern, so users can test
whether a method assigns intensity to the correct physical source rather than
only reducing residual error. The paired clean, perturbed, OOD, and
identifiability views make failure modes measurable: learnability, shortcut use,
acquisition sensitivity, component ambiguity, and principled abstention are
separated instead of collapsed into one reconstruction score.

Every source row receives a stable ID, canonical structure hash, composition,
quality result, and eligibility flags. Derived labels are deterministic,
versioned functions of formal structures. Rejected rows remain accounted for.
Perturbed views inherit their clean parent's group and cannot cross a split.
Thus reconstruction can be scored independently from recovery of $z$ or
$\{c_k\}$.

\section{HEA-SRO Data}

The source corpus contains ordered cells and special quasirandom structures
(SQS) with up to 125 atoms
from Al, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Mn, Ni, and Si
~\cite{li2024dataset,li2024modeling}. Of 84,024 source rows, 84,019 pass quality
checks and five are rejected. Each accepted structure has one immutable clean
Cu K$\alpha$ pattern with 4,001 points. Perturbations are indexed as views, not
additional structures.

For species $a,b$ and shell $s$, the primary target is
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ab}^{(s)}=1-\frac{P(b\mid a,s)}{c_b},
\end{equation}
\begin{revision}
where $c_b$ is the global concentration. For a present central species, a
defined neighbor shell, and $c_b>0$, a zero $a\!\to\!b$ count gives
$P(b\mid a,s)=0$ and hence $\alpha_{ab}^{(s)}=1$; it is not missing data.
Absent species and undefined shells remain masked. The corrected overlay
adds 669,370 previously omitted targets across 88,019 rows (84,019 source
structures and 4,000 counterfactuals), without changing the frozen splits.
An independent implementation agrees on 1,819 checked rows. This validates
label arithmetic under the declared shell convention, not bulk-alloy SRO
or the physical sufficiency of the XRD simulator. Each target remains
linked to its finite structure. Exact atom-index recovery is not a task.
\end{revision}

not a universal non-identifiability result. Family labels remain automated
diagnostics rather than adjudicated phase truth.
\end{revision}

\section{Gamma/Gamma-Prime Decomposition Data}

Gamma-Decomp is the stable task ID for this \gphase{}/\gp{} track. The generator
pairs FCC \gphase{} and \lone{} \gp{} structures and varies phase
weight, broadening, strain, crystallite size, background, zero shift, and noise
within declared ranges. Physically relaxed and parameterized counterfactual
pairs are explicitly distinguished. Component truth is stored before summation.

Gamma-Decomp v1.1 contains 10,000 mixtures, 21,116 windows, 175,150 raw
reflection-provenance rows, and 165,150 full-pattern scoring components. The
window view has 34,983 scoring components. Strictly coincident \gp{} $(221)$ and
$(300)$ reflections remain separate in provenance but merge into an equivalent
HKL scoring group. Seventeen phase/HKL groups are evaluated.

\begin{revision}
Scoring difficulty is computed from eligible targets after merging
equivalent components. Context reflections do not define the scoring
bucket. For each eligible pair, the separation ratio is
\[
\rho_{ij}=|m_i-m_j|/\{(w_i+w_j)/2\},
\]
where $m$ is peak center and $w$ is full width at half maximum (FWHM).
Zero-target windows are empty; one-target windows are single. For at least
two targets, $\rho_{\min}=\min_{i<j}\rho_{ij}$ defines severe if
$\rho_{\min}<0.5$, strong if $0.5\le\rho_{\min}<1$, partial if
$1\le\rho_{\min}<2$, and resolved if $\rho_{\min}\ge2$. Thus ``severe
overlap'' means peak centers are separated by less than half the average FWHM,
not an aesthetic choice made after seeing model errors. Released context
buckets are retained as provenance and must not substitute for these
target-based buckets.
\end{revision}

HEA groups structure hashes, parents, and all observation views before assigning
ID, composition OOD, chemistry OOD, and SRO OOD splits. Gamma groups parent
pairs and views before instrument and overlap slices. Nearest-neighbor audits
cover structure and spectral spaces. An ordinary random split is not presented
as evidence of new-chemistry or acquisition generalization.

HEA macro-averages masked MAE within a parent and then across parents. Relative
improvement over mandatory Composition Ridge is
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{SRO}}=100\max(0,1-\mathrm{MAE}_{model}/\mathrm{MAE}_{base}).
\end{equation}
\begin{revision}
Per-shell Spearman correlation pools valid targets within each shell; it is
not a parent-macro statistic. We retain the original SRO-OOD membership as
\emph{legacy SRO OOD}: its label-shift interpretation has not been
re-established under the corrected mask.
Gamma's protocol requires component F1, joint phase/HKL recovery, count
accuracy, and matched center/width/area/profile errors. Its complete revised
matrix remains pending. Matching requires equal phase and equivalent HKL,
with center error at most $\max(0.15^\circ,1.5w_{\rm true})$; the cost
combines normalized center, width, and profile errors. Appendix~\ref{app:gamma}
defines aggregation and empty-target handling. Resampling follows the
bootstrap principle~\cite{efron1994bootstrap}, with analysis-specific
replicate counts stated below. No cross-track composite score is defined
because SRO regression and unordered component recovery use different targets.
\end{revision}

\section{Benchmark Validation}

\begin{revision}
\subsection{Corrected HEA Results}
All HEA results in Tables~\ref{tab:primary_results}--\ref{tab:signal_controls}
use corrected labels, frozen original splits, and the original clean
observations. Table~\ref{tab:primary_results} reports \emph{retraining}, not
rescoring of old predictions. Composition Ridge is deterministic; the other
formal models record seeds 2027--2031. XGBoost yields identical predictions
across these seeds, so its zero standard deviation (SD) does not represent five distinct
stochastic fits. Appendix~\ref{app:settings} gives training settings.

Spectrum Transformer has the lowest mean MAE among the four formal models
on every split. On ID it improves over Composition Ridge from 0.5310 to
0.4156 (21.73\%), while Peak XGBoost reaches 0.4402 and ResNet1D 0.5121.
Thus spectral predictors are not interchangeable: a neural architecture
alone does not guarantee a large gain, and handcrafted peak descriptors
remain competitive. Error is highest on the legacy SRO-OOD membership
for every model; this is an empirical difficulty comparison, not renewed
validation of that split's SRO-shift semantics. The exploratory
physics-constrained variant improves Transformer MAE by only
0.0003--0.0024; its complete metrics are reported separately in
Table~\ref{tab:extended_hea}, without a claim of significant superiority.

\subsection{Incremental Value Beyond Composition}
Comparing different model families cannot isolate the value of the input.
We therefore fit the same XGBoost learner with composition alone (C),
98 peak descriptors alone (X), and their concatenation (C+X), using the
same splits, training budget, and seed 2027. Table~\ref{tab:signal_controls}
shows the C+X versus C contrast. ID MAE decreases from 0.5230 to 0.4077,
with paired difference $-0.1153$ and 95\% parent-bootstrap interval
$[-0.1180,-0.1125]$. Reductions span 19.90--22.56\% across four splits.
The 10,000-resample intervals (bootstrap seed 20261002) condition on one
fitted seed; they do not measure training-seed variation. This is a
post-review feature ablation, not a preregistered confirmatory test.

These gains establish predictive information beyond composition under the
released clean simulator, not a direct diffuse-scattering SRO signature.
With composition, cell, and positions fixed, the Transformer reduces
error relative to a zero-change predictor by only 4.84--7.44\%; C+X
does not improve that paired-change target (Table~\ref{tab:fixed_cell}).
In an additional matched ID diagnostic, the old perturbation generator
increases C+X MAE from 0.2751 to 0.6260. The renderer was subsequently
corrected to preserve integrated peak area; matched reevaluation is not
complete. The old negative result therefore demonstrates sensitivity to
that generator, not a calibrated experimental noise limit.

\subsection{Gamma Evaluation Status}
The revised evaluator distinguishes eligible targets from context, handles
empty windows, and removes arbitrary component IDs from physical matching
and tied-risk calculations. However, the full prediction inventory,
adaptation, and corrected uncertainty analysis are incomplete. Historical
Gamma F1 scores are therefore not carried into the updated main table.
A constrained peak-profile fit is not a Rietveld refinement baseline;
neither a validated Rietveld comparison nor the requested known-count,
known-phase, and known-instrument models is available. This limits the
current benchmark evaluation, rather than being resolved by the HEA gains.
\end{revision}

\href{https://github.com/guohajining-cmd/alloy-xrd-bench/releases/tag/v1.1.2}{v1.1.2 release}
and the Hugging Face
\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/jimi7771/alloy-xrd-bench/tree/v1.1.2}{v1.1.2 dataset revision}.
The original data and frozen split membership are retained; the correction
does not establish completion of the outstanding validation described below.
\end{revision}

The computational structure is ground truth for generated samples, but does not
prove that experimental powder XRD uniquely determines occupancy. Periodic
supercells, similar scattering factors, finite resolution, texture, microstrain,
nanostructure, and unmodeled phases can make inversion ambiguous. Family labels
are automated and unadjudicated. Occupancy uses simulated perturbations only;
real repeated-measurement import and noise calibration are v1.2 gates.

\begin{revision}
The experimental data included in this release do not provide independent
SRO or reflection-level component labels. The HEA experimental set supports mother-phase trends
~\cite{wang2021heaf}. The Ni-based sanity set uses public raw arrays for
heat-treated IN718 and AM718R, where \gp{} and gamma-double-prime superlattice
reflections are not distinguishable
~\cite{markanday2022repairdata,markanday2022repair}. It supports phase-level and
whole-pattern checks, not experimental component-level validation.
The distinction between \gp{} and gamma-double-prime is important: Alloy 718
precipitation and dual-superlattice alloy studies concern distinct precipitate
structures~\cite{devaux2008gamma,mignanelli2017dual}, not interchangeable
ground-truth labels for this two-phase benchmark.
\end{revision}

\begin{revision}
The current forward model has not been validated for recovering an explicit
diffuse-scattering SRO signal. Finite-cell label arithmetic also does not
quantify convergence to bulk order: neither a supercell-size study nor
independent configuration-sampling uncertainty is complete. Parent-bootstrap
intervals describe predictive-error variation, not these physical or label
uncertainties.

\dataset{} provides a first-stage evaluation resource for future alloy
powder-XRD refinement models, with complementary local-order and
component-decomposition subproblems. Corrected HEA experiments show incremental predictive value
over composition on clean simulated observations, but substantially weaker
fixed-cell evidence and sensitivity to the old perturbation generator.
The contribution is a traceable evaluation resource with explicit failure
boundaries, not a new universal inverse-problem principle or demonstrated
experimental SRO recovery. Completing corrected Gamma evaluation and
external physical validation is necessary before making broader claims
about benchmark maturity or experimental transfer.
A joint refinement pipeline remains future work.
\end{revision}

\section{Training and Statistical Protocol}
\label{app:settings}

Each model is fit on the training partition of the declared split; neural
checkpoint selection uses validation parent-macro MAE. Test observations
are not used for checkpoint selection. Networks use masked target loss,
AdamW, learning rate $10^{-3}$, at most 40 epochs, and patience 6.
ResNet1D and Transformer use batch size 128; the exploratory physics
variant uses 64, 16 reconstructed peaks, and reconstruction weight 0.1.
Ridge uses regularization $\alpha=1$. Peak XGBoost uses at most 32 peaks,
300 estimators, depth 6, and learning rate 0.05. The feature ablation uses
the same tree budget, CPU histogram training, and one seed (2027).
Its recorded environment is Python 3.11.15, NumPy 2.4.6, and XGBoost 3.2.0.
These settings describe completed experiments, not newly tuned models.

For parent $p$, we first average absolute error over defined targets of
each sample, then over samples of that parent, and finally across held-out
parents. The paired bootstrap draws the same parent IDs with replacement
for both models, recomputes the mean difference, and takes percentile
endpoints. All C versus C+X comparisons use 10,000 replicates and bootstrap
seed 20261002. The evidence also stores 98.75\% intervals allocating error
over these four splits; neither interval family corrects all exploratory
contrasts. Across-seed SD and parent-bootstrap intervals answer different
questions and are not substituted for each other.

\section{Gamma Scoring Contract and Outstanding Evidence}
\label{app:gamma}
Eligible truth components are grouped by sample. Phase/HKL-gated optimal
bipartite matching gives true positives (TP), false positives (FP), and
false negatives (FN). Counts are pooled within each parent pair before
forming F1, $2\mathrm{TP}/(2\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}+\mathrm{FN})$;
parent scores are then macro-averaged. A zero denominator gives zero F1.
The field named phase/HKL accuracy is joint recovery,
$\mathrm{TP}/\max(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN},\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP},1)$,
not separate phase and HKL classification accuracy. Count accuracy is
the fraction of samples with exactly correct component count within a
parent, macro-averaged over parents. Empty-target cases are therefore
retained even though their F1 convention differs from count accuracy.

Center, width, integrated-area, and profile errors apply to matched
components and must be read alongside F1 to avoid rewarding selective
detection. The revised forward-reconstruction reference contains only
eligible component curves; it is not the observed whole pattern including
context/background. Tied confidence risk uses expected uniform
tie permutations. Sorting by component ID is not a physical tie-break.

These definitions are now explicit, but definitions and regression tests
do not replace a completed experiment. The outstanding matrix must cover
both full patterns and windows, all required metrics, eligible-target
difficulty, instrument slices, empty cases, and uncertainty that handles
shared structures across parent pairs. Traditional whole-pattern and
privileged-information baselines remain missing. No corrected numerical
Gamma ranking or superiority claim is asserted in this revision.
